16. CONTENT EXPERIENCE FRAMEWORK
================================================================================

THREE PILLARS (Content strategy — frog/Capgemini):

1. Audience — Design around mode and moment of need
2. Content Qualities & Types — GEO/LLM surfacing, high-impact formats
3. Content Orchestration — Activation across owned, third-party, and ecosystem
                          channels

---

AUDIENCE BEHAVIOR MODES:

Ad-hoc information searchers (🔴 HIGH PRIORITY)
- Seek quick, reliable answers to specific scientific questions in flow of work
- Engage briefly; expect clarity, credibility, immediate relevance

Evidence explorers (🔴 HIGH PRIORITY)
- Go beyond quick answers to explore implications, validate understanding
- Revisit content, follow citations, work through evidence application

Scientific interpreters (🟡 IMPLICATED)
- Critically analyse, compare, contextualise scientific evidence
- Seek rigour, transparency, access to primary data

Patient-facing translators (🟡 LOW PRIORITY)
- Prepare to explain scientific information in patient conversations
- Reuse trusted content for shared decision-making

Mastery-driven learners (🔵 DE-PRIORITIZE)
- Build deep, structured understanding over time
- Willing to invest attention; progress through complexity
- Seek formal learning via objective industry bodies; dubious of pharma-led content

---

HIGH-PRIORITY CONTENT TYPES:

Core research with layered depth
- Pre-generated key insights (3-5 bullets at top)
- Clear structure: summary → explanation → evidence → deeper links
- Inline citations in first screen
- Visible provenance (last updated, author/reviewer role, evidence type/level)
- Time signal; structured tags for AI classification
- Direct links to trusted external evidence
- Clinical applicability translation

Short-form video
- 3-5 minutes MAXIMUM
- Visually-led (flow diagrams, mechanisms, steps)
- Skippable + speed-controlled + closed-captioned + FULL TRANSCRIPT
- Segmented chapters; clear time signal
- Highlighted key insights

Infographics & quick visuals
- Designed for rapid visual understanding (seconds, not minutes)
- Must include text equivalents/structured annotations
- Built on-site (not static images) for AI readability
- Expandable sections (no forced reading)
- Extractable text layer

AI retrieval chat
- Answer-first interaction
- RETRIEVAL-BASED (grounded in governed MedVerse content, NOT generative)
- Cited responses with clear sources + confidence signals
- Pre-defined prompts to guide common queries
- Follow-up questions + suggested queries

Patient-ready explainers
- Simplified language
- Inline citations maintaining traceability
- Ready-to-share outputs for use in real patient interactions
- Clearly delineated from HCP-only content
- Grounded in same governed MedVerse evidence

---

LOWER-PRIORITY CONTENT TYPES:

Long-form e-learning modules: Heavy investment; pharma-skeptical; not AI-legible
PDF static documents: Not AI-readable; too dense for most HCP moments
Long-form audio/podcast: Too time-consuming; already well-served by other sources
Long-form traditional research formats: "No more than 3-5 minutes... anything
                                        longer is too much"

---

AI-NATIVE CONTENT STRUCTURING — 4 PRINCIPLES:

1. Open, crawlable pages
   - No login barriers; full content served without authentication
   - Tag where attestation requirements don't exist
   - All major LLMs rely on publicly indexed pages

2. Clear structure
   - Single H1 = canonical title
   - Summary paragraph after H1
   - Semantic <section> tags linked to headings
   - H3 subsection headings for narrow query retrieval

3. Machine-readable formats
   - HTML-first data (not images of tables or PDFs)
   - Machine-readable dates
   - Structured metadata with evidence type classification
   - Each reference classified by study type

4. Citation-ready design
   - DOI-linked inline citations
   - Full reference list in HTML (not PDF appendix)
   - Schema markup in <head>
   - Explicitly marked-up key takeaway boxes

---

CONTENT ORCHESTRATION — ECOSYSTEM MODEL:

MedVerse must operate as a SCIENTIFIC INTELLIGENCE LAYER within a broader
ecosystem — not a standalone destination.

HCP Discovery Journey:
1. Discovery starts on LLMs and Search (general + medical)
2. Validation happens in trusted medical environments (UpToDate, journals)
3. Action happens in time-constrained moments
4. HCPs still turn to field reps for pharma-led information

Ecosystem Channels:
- General LLMs: Gemini, Claude, ChatGPT, Apple Intelligence
- Medical LLMs: OpenEvidence, UpToDate, MedAI, DermAI, Heidi, Doximity, etc.
- Journals & Medical Outlets: Medscape, PubMed, NEJM, JAMA
- MedVerse: Core pull destination; standardized medical information across TAs;
           bi-directional links to program sites; LLM & third-party ready
- Linked program sites: ADVENT, IMMERSE, Br1dge, RDU, Nerve Nexus — retain
                       branded identity
- Other owned channels: Social, congress content, field reps

Best-in-Class Orchestration Principles:
- Minimize login friction — attestation pop-ups deter AI crawlers
- Personalization on trusted platforms HCPs already use
- LLM visibility = content quality and structure
- Social ≠ primary channel for core HCP behavior modes
- Third-party partnerships critical (OpenEvidence, UpToDate, API connectors)
- Field teams = trust + activation layer

17. HCP RESEARCH FINDINGS
================================================================================

RESEARCH SCOPE:

12 HCPs interviewed (recruitment spec: RES-005 — Future-Ready Content Research Screener V1, May 12, 2026)
Markets: US, France, Germany
Profiles: Generalists + specialists; early-career → experienced; range of digital/AI
         adoption

---

5 KEY INSIGHTS:

INSIGHT 1: AI Use Is Widespread — But Segmented by Task
Orientation · memory refresh · clinical reasoning (hypothesis support only).
Pattern: AI → cross-check → decision. Human judgement remains final authority.

INSIGHT 2: Trust Collapses When AI Overreaches
Single negative experience can cause tool abandonment. Hallucinations viewed
more negatively than stated limitations.

INSIGHT 3: Pharma Content Trusted for Clinical Decisioning — NOT Learning
~90% of HCPs interviewed would NOT trust pharma for traditional learning modules.

INSIGHT 4: UpToDate Remains the Gold Standard
Used at least once per shift by some HCPs. HCPs start with Google/AI summaries,
then move to UpToDate to confirm/expand.

INSIGHT 5: Visual Structure Consistently Outperforms Narrative
HCPs scan for answers — they do not read sequentially.

---

5 IMPLICATIONS FOR MEDVERSE:

1. Don't try to replace UpToDate — position as Sanofi-specific layer
2. Focus where Sanofi can differentiate (product info, device training, access)
3. Treat external LLMs as primary discovery channels
4. Build AI-enabled hub to RAG all Sanofi content with visible citations
5. Gating is a liability unless it clearly adds value
